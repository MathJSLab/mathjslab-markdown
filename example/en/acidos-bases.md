# Acids and bases: proton transfer

**Objective:** identify an acid and its conjugate base in an equation.
**Extensions:** MathJax, SMILES.

## Key ideas

In the Brønsted–Lowry model, an acid donates a proton and a base accepts it.
Ethanoic acid can transfer a proton to water:

$$
\ce{CH3COOH + H2O <=> CH3COO- + H3O+}.
$$

The acid and ethanoate ion form a conjugate pair, differing by one proton.
Water acts as a base in this reaction. The reversible arrow indicates
equilibrium, not complete conversion of all acid molecules.

```smiles
CC(=O)O
```

```smiles
CC(=O)[O-]
```

Compare the neutral molecule with the ion, whose oxygen has an explicit charge
in SMILES. “Strong” describes the extent of ionization in a medium;
“concentrated” describes the amount per volume. They are different concepts.
This note analyzes representations without proposing reagent handling.

## Practice

1. What is ethanoic acid’s conjugate base?
2. Which species accepted the proton in the equation?

## Answers with explanations

1. The ethanoate ion, $\ce{CH3COO-}$.
2. Water, forming $\ce{H3O+}$.
