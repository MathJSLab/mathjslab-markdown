# Esterification: reactants, products and equilibrium

**Objective:** interpret ester formation and conserve atoms. **Extensions:**
MathJax with mhchem and SMILES.

## A reversible transformation

Ethanoic acid and ethanol can form ethyl ethanoate and water. Fischer
esterification takes place under acid catalysis and is reversible:

$$
\ce{CH3COOH + C2H5OH <=> CH3COOC2H5 + H2O}.
$$

The catalyst speeds up the approach to equilibrium without changing its
constant at the same temperature. Removing water or using excess reactant can
favor ester formation. This scheme is for conceptual study.

### Ethanoic acid

```smiles
CC(=O)O
```

### Ethanol

```smiles
CCO
```

### Ethyl ethanoate

```smiles
CC(=O)OCC
```

## Checking conservation

The reactants contain four carbons, ten hydrogens and three oxygens. The ester
has formula $\ce{C4H8O2}$, and water completes the count. Conserving atoms is
necessary but not sufficient to guarantee that an equation represents a
reaction that actually occurs.

## Practice

1. Which functional group appears in the organic product?
2. Does a catalyst, by itself, increase the amount of product at equilibrium?
3. Compare the oxygen counts on the two sides.

## Answers with explanations

1. An ester, with the $\ce{-COO-}$ group.
2. No; it changes the rate, not the equilibrium constant.
3. Each side has three: two in the acid and one in the alcohol; two in the
   ester and one in water.
