# Carbon dioxide: bonds and symmetry

**Objective:** connect a linear structure with dipole cancellation.
**Extensions:** molecule-3d, SMILES, MathJax.

## Key ideas

Carbon dioxide has formula $\ce{CO2}$. Carbon forms two double bonds, one to
each oxygen. In SMILES, each `=` denotes a double bond; the representation
shows connectivity, not electron distribution.

```smiles
O=C=O
```

```xyz
3
Linear CO2: approximate model
O -1.160 0.000 0.000
C 0.000 0.000 0.000
O 1.160 0.000 0.000
```

The XYZ model uses approximate coordinates in ångströms and makes the linear
geometry visible. There are two bonding electron regions around carbon,
separated by approximately $180^\circ$. Each C–O bond is polar, but their
dipoles point in opposite directions and cancel in the symmetrical molecule.

A nonpolar molecule can have polar bonds. Molecular polarity also depends on
geometry. Compare with water, whose bent shape prevents this cancellation.

## Practice

1. How many atoms are in one CO₂ molecule?
2. Does a double bond count as two regions in geometry analysis?

## Answers with explanations

1. Three: one carbon and two oxygens.
2. No; it counts as one bonding region.
