# Isomerism: same formula, different structures

**Objective:** distinguish chain isomerism from functional-group isomerism.
**Extensions:** SMILES and MathJax.

## A formula is not a map of bonds

Compounds with formula $\ce{C4H10}$ can have a straight or branched chain.
Compare butane and 2-methylpropane:

```smiles
CCCC
```

```smiles
CC(C)C
```

Both have four carbons and ten hydrogens, but their connectivity differs. This
is classified as chain isomerism.

## Functional-group isomerism

Ethanol and methoxymethane share the formula $\ce{C2H6O}$. The first has a
hydroxyl group; the second has an oxygen between carbons.

```smiles
CCO
```

```smiles
COC
```

Ethanol's ability to donate hydrogen bonds helps explain differences in
physical properties between these isomers. A molecular formula alone does not
specify all these properties.

## Practice

1. Count butane's hydrogens using carbon's usual valence of 4.
2. Does changing the orientation of a butane drawing create another isomer?
3. What information is missing from a molecular formula when identifying these
   pairs?

## Answers with explanations

1. Terminal carbons have three H each; internal ones have two: $3+2+2+3=10$.
2. No. Rotating the drawing without changing bonds preserves connectivity.
3. How the atoms are bonded; in other cases, spatial configuration is also
   needed.
